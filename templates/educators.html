CipherBreak EDU for Educators

A proposed educational-technology product for university cryptography courses.

The problem

DES combines permutations, key transformations, binary manipulation, S-box substitutions and 16 Feistel rounds. Static slides show the names of these steps, but students rarely see how the values actually change — and instructors struggle to demonstrate intermediate calculations or to check who really understood them.

The solution

An escape-room game where every lock is a real DES operation, computed by a traced DES engine. Students learn by doing, get immediate targeted feedback, and can always inspect the full 16-round trace. Instructors get classrooms, learning-objective analytics and a lecture demonstration mode.

How the four rooms teach DES

RoomStudents practise
1 · Hex GateASCII → hex, the 64-bit block, Initial Permutation, splitting into L0/R0
2 · Key VaultParity & 56 effective bits, PC-1, C0/D0, the shift schedule, PC-2, all 16 round keys
3 · Round ReactorExpansion E, XOR with the round key, S-box row/column lookup, P, the Feistel update, the final swap and IP⁻¹
4 · Decryption ChamberReversed key order, a decryption round, recovering the plaintext, why DES is obsolete

Sample 20–30 minute class activity

  1. (3 min) Students join your classroom with the join code and take the optional pre-assessment.
  2. (5 min) Instructor demo mode: walk through one round on the projector with the classic test vector.
  3. (15 min) Students play Rooms 1–3 in Guided mode in pairs.
  4. (5 min) Review the class "most missed concepts" chart together; assign Room 4 + post-assessment as homework.

Proposed business model

  • Instructor classroom trial.

  • Additional cryptography modules (e.g. AES, modes of operation, RSA) in future versions.

These are proposed opportunities for a student project. We do not claim existing customers, revenue, partnerships or published research results.

Value

Students: engaging, immediate feedback, personalised practice, learning by doing, revisit hard concepts any time.

Professors: interactive lecture demonstrations, ready-made activities, automated practice, outcome monitoring, visibility into common misconceptions.
